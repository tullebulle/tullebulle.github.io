Scalable Beckmann Maps improve one-step generation

Ulrik Unneberg1,⋆, Shiyi Wang1,⋆, Lee Cheuk-Kit1, Felix Berg2 Boris Kozinsky1, Yilun Du1, Michael Albergo1, Eric Vanden-Eijnden3,4

1Harvard University, 2Imperial College London, 3New York University, 4Capital Fund Management

Preprint, 2026. ⋆Core contributors.

Abstract. Beckmann Transport Models (BTM) learn one-step generative maps end-to-end from data, without a teacher, a distillation stage, or pretrained features. We introduce Scalable Beckmann Maps (SBM), which bring to BTM the two ingredients behind the quality of modern multistep generators: classifier-free guidance (CFG) and a training recipe that scales, while keeping sampling at a single network evaluation. CFG combines conditional and unconditional velocity or score predictions, which a Beckmann map does not have. Our guidance rests on one observation: the BTM current is a positive multiple of an autonomous denoising field, to which CFG applies as it does to a velocity. With exact denoisers this gives guided BTM. In practice we substitute the map’s own conditional and null predictions for the denoisers, which requires no additional network and introduces a bias that we characterize. We show that the guided update is consistent with a guided conservation equation, and that guidance can move the boundaries between basins, sending more noise to the modes a condition favors. Trained from scratch in the latent of a representation autoencoder, SBM reaches a one-step FID of 1.6 on ImageNet 256×256 without a teacher or a feature loss, and a GenEval score of 0.86 in text-to-image generation with one network evaluation. As a world model, where we train without guidance, the recipe alone outperforms DriftWorld on all five metrics of two robot-manipulation benchmarks, with 2.4–5.4× less training compute and no pretrained auxiliary feature encoder.

Summary

  1. The problem. Beckmann Transport Models learn a one-step map end-to-end from data, with no teacher, no second prediction head and no pretrained feature network, but they have lacked the two ingredients behind modern generators: a mature training recipe and classifier-free guidance. The original BTM reads a one-step FID of 17.6 on ImageNet 256, where recent one-step generators are below 2. CFG combines conditional and unconditional velocity or score predictions, and a map has neither.
  2. Guidance and a training recipe for one-step maps. We guide the map during training with its own conditional and null predictions. The BTM current is a positive multiple of an autonomous denoising field, to which CFG applies as it does to a velocity; substituting the map’s predictions for the denoisers needs no additional network and keeps sampling at one network evaluation. A recipe for training at scale comes with it.
  3. Theoretical interpretation and guarantees. We derive the guidance rule through the autonomous denoising interpretation of BTM and characterize the bias of substituting map predictions for denoisers. For the idealized population objective the guided stopped update is consistent with a guided conservation equation, and a two-mode construction shows how guidance moves basin boundaries and raises the probability of the modes a condition favours.
  4. Performant one-step generation across tasks. SBM-XL, trained from scratch in the latent of a representation autoencoder, reaches a one-step FID of 1.61 on ImageNet 256×256 without a teacher or a feature loss, the lowest we know of for a feature-free one-step model at any budget. In text-to-image generation SBM reaches a GenEval score of 0.856 with one network evaluation. On BridgeData V2 and RT-1 the world models use neither guidance nor a feature loss and beat every reported baseline on all five metrics, with 2.4 to 5.4× less training compute than DriftWorld.

Guidance for one-step Beckmann maps

A Beckmann transport model learns the endpoint map T of a time-independent current J: the map is constant along the flow lines of the current (J·∇T = 0 off the data) and is the identity on the data manifold. BTM enforces this with a stop-gradient transport loss and a boundary loss. Neither term ever shows the map what a wrong image for a given class looks like.

The current is a denoising field. With the linear interpolant IS = (1 − S)x0 + S x1 and S uniform, the conditional current factors as Jc = hc (z̅c − id) with hc > 0, where z̅c is a weighted posterior mean of the data: an autonomous denoiser. The current is a positive multiple of a denoising field, so CFG applies to it as it does to a velocity.

Implicit CFG on the maps. Classifier-free guidance combines the conditional and null denoisers; in implicit form the guided denoiser is z̅c + (1 − ω−1)(z̅cω − z̅∅). Substituting the map’s guided and null endpoint predictions (T, T∅) for the denoisers gives a guided transport direction. No additional denoiser is trained: both predictions come from one network through condition dropout.

BTM’s stopped update. Apply the BTM stop-gradient update to the guided direction, and the same correction to the clean regression target of the boundary loss. The current now depends on the map we seek, and we ask for a map that predicts the endpoint of its own current: self-consistency.

Guided transport direction.

The factor 1 − ω−1 comes from implicit CFG, and 1 / (1 − S) converts endpoint displacements into interpolant velocities. The green term is the only change from BTM; for ω > 1 it follows the conditional–null disagreement at IS, while the interpolation path is unchanged. With exact denoisers in place of the maps, the induced velocity is a positive rescaling of the autonomous CFG field; with the maps, the substitution error of the field is (1 − ω−1)[(T − z̅cω) − (T∅ − z̅∅)], a mismatch of the conditional–null contrasts in which the two individual errors can cancel. At fixed input and time the implicit correction preserves the conditional covariance of the BTM target, whereas the explicit guided target ωİS − (ω − 1)(T∅(IS) − IS)/(1 − S) scales it by ω2: at ω = 5 its minibatch-gradient variance is 85 times larger and the map reads 66 against 21 FID at 20 epochs.

Guided training losses.

The stop-gradient freezes the entire target in each term, including the guidance correction. At ω = 1 both losses reduce to ordinary conditional BTM.

Theorem (consistency of the guided stopped update). Let 1 ≤ ω < ∞ and λ > 0, let the class data lie on a smooth lower-dimensional manifold, and let the null map fix the data. Then the population stopped update of the guided losses vanishes against every smooth compactly supported perturbation exactly when the map satisfies the guided conservation equation

so a stationary map predicts the endpoint of its own guided current. Sufficiently weak guidance admits a local self-consistent endpoint map near the data manifold, close to the ordinary conditional BTM map, and the guided equations permit a stronger preference for a condition-favoured mode while keeping the data support (Figure 2). The theorem characterizes the idealized population update with a clean boundary loss; it does not establish uniqueness of the guided map or convergence of network training.

Noisy boundary supervision. In practice the conditional and null maps are branches of one network Tθ(x, c, γ) trained with condition dropout, and the boundary loss evaluates the map at a noisy input Iγ = (1 − γ)x0 + γx1 while keeping x1 as the clean target; γ is the boundary level and also a network input. Its boundary-only fixed point is guided denoising supervision with the null branch as reference:

So at γ = s the map acts as a guided denoiser of Is, and at a fixed read level γread it predicts the flow endpoint from pure noise, which is how it samples: x = Tθ(x0, c, γread).

Algorithm 1 Simple SBM training (ω = 1 is BTM)
for each training step:
    draw x0 ~ N(0, I),  (x1, c) ~ data,  S ~ U(0, 1);  replace c by ∅ with a fixed probability
    I_S = (1 - S) x0 + S x1
    v   = x1 - x0 + (1 - 1/ω) / (1 - S) * (T(I_S, c) - T(I_S, ∅))          # guided direction
    L_tr = | T(I_S, c) - sg[ T(I_S, c) + DT(I_S, c) v ] |^2
    L_bd = | T(x1, c)  - sg[ x1 + (1 - 1/ω) (T(x1, c) - T(x1, ∅)) ] |^2
    θ ← θ - η ∇θ ( L_tr + λ L_bd )

sample:  x = T(x0, c)                                               # one network call, no guidance

The full recipe (Algorithm 2 in the paper) adds the boundary level γ, a frozen copy of the network for the stopped targets, a per-row normalization of the transport loss, an adaptive gradient balancer and a mixture of transport times; Figure 8 shows what each of these is worth.

Text-to-image

SBM-B and SBM-XL are trained from scratch on the training data of MiniT2I (Wang et al., 2026), with its schedule and its frozen FLAN-T5-Large text encoder, and evaluated on GenEval with one map evaluation and one decoder call per image. The maps act in the latent of a frozen DINOv2-S encoder and are decoded at 256 px, whereas the baselines are pixel-space systems at 512 px, with compute as estimated by the MiniT2I authors.

With a single network evaluation, SBM comes within a few GenEval points of multi-step flow models trained on the same data, at about one hundredth of their sampling cost. SBM-XL reaches 0.856 and SBM-B 0.833, at 0.57 and 0.33 TFLOP per image. At matched training compute this is around 0.03 below the 100-step MiniT2I-B/16 and L/16 models, which use 200 network evaluations per image and 140 and 470 times the inference compute of SBM-XL. The four-step MeanFlow distillation of MiniT2I-B/16 scores 0.842, between the two SBM models, but costs 2.9 times the inference compute of SBM-XL and 4.9 times that of SBM-B, and needs a separate distillation stage. Both SBM models also score above the other pixel-space systems except DeCo. Figure 1 shows uncurated samples next to MiniT2I-L/16.

Try it

The SBM-XL text-to-image map, live: type a prompt and the map returns eight images, one network evaluation each, from the Space on Hugging Face ZeroGPU. The first request after idle time wakes the Space, which takes about a minute; later ones return in about a second. Full-sentence prompts work best. Without a Hugging Face account the GPU allows a few runs a day per address; for more, open the demo on Hugging Face and sign in. Eight fixed seeds for twelve prompts, with nothing selected, are in the seed gallery.

World modelling

Action-conditioned next-frame prediction on BridgeData V2 and RT-1 under DriftWorld’s protocol (256×256, validation split): given four frames and their actions, the map samples the next frame with one network evaluation, and longer videos are generated autoregressively in chunks of eight frames with the history reset to the ground truth at the start of each chunk. Frames live in the SD3 autoencoder latent. All SBM world models use ω = 1 and no auxiliary feature loss: the recipe alone does the work. Baseline rows are taken from DriftWorld’s Table IV; compute is the training compute of the generator in EFLOP, NFE the number of network evaluations per generation call, and inference time is per generated frame on one GPU (baselines H100 as reported; SBM H200, batch 1, bf16).

On Bridge-V2, SBM beats DriftWorld on all five metrics at 2.4× less training compute, and without the DINOv3 feature encoder that DriftWorld’s own ablation finds essential. On RT-1 the short run passes DriftWorld on all five metrics at 5.4× less compute, and the long run at 43.4 EFLOP is past every published entry of the table. The FVD values are 2.5× to 3.9× below DriftWorld’s: past the scored eight frames, with the model fed its own frames, DriftWorld’s arm dissolves within about ten frames while the SBM rollout keeps the arm solid and completes the task (Figure 7).

Ablations: from BTM to SBM

Figure 8 shows what each part of the recipe is worth, at base size and 20 epochs, for DiT-B/2 maps trained from scratch in the SD-VAE latent. The BTM configuration we started from reads an FID of 75.6. Four changes that do not involve guidance bring it to 46.7, the native BTM of Figure 3: a smaller batch, a late transport-time distribution, the ADM latent preprocessing and the learning-rate drop of the native recipe. Guidance is the change that matters most: the guided targets, together with the smoothed gradient balancer, take the map from 46.7 to 20.7 at the same epoch budget and to 25.9 at the same FLOP budget. The unguided changes that come with the recipe net to zero. Without the per-row normalization of the transport loss the map reads worse than native BTM; removing any other component costs less than eight points.
